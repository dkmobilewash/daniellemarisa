If you want a downtown Albuquerque wedding with real history behind it, Hotel Andaluz is the venue most local planners think of first. Originally opened in 1939 as one of Conrad Hilton's earliest hotels, the building was restored in 2004 into a Spanish Revival showpiece — and it holds the distinction of being one of the first historic hotels in the world to earn LEED Gold certification. For couples who want their wedding photos to have architectural weight — carved wood, wrought iron, arched windows — without leaving the city, it's hard to beat.

## What makes Hotel Andaluz special

The design language here is unmistakably Andalusian: horseshoe arches, hand-painted tile, dark wood balconies overlooking a central lobby, and a rooftop lounge with sweeping views of the Sandia Mountains and the Downtown skyline. Because it's a full-service hotel in the middle of Downtown Albuquerque, it solves two problems at once — a genuinely beautiful backdrop, and built-in lodging for out-of-town guests who can walk out of the elevator and into your reception.

It also reads differently depending on the season and time of day. Morning light through the lobby's stained-glass skylight is soft and warm; the rooftop at golden hour turns the whole building amber. Couples who want a wedding that feels like Albuquerque's history — not just its scenery — tend to gravitate here over newer, more generic ballroom venues.

## Capacity & logistics

Hotel Andaluz offers five distinct indoor event spaces, so the venue scales from an intimate rehearsal dinner to a full 200-guest reception without ever feeling like a re-purposed conference room:

- **Barcelona Ballroom** — the primary reception space, holding up to 200 guests, with large windows, a private terrace, and gold chiavari chairs included.
- **Casablanca Room** — a smaller, more intimate room for up to 70 guests, well suited to a rehearsal dinner or a wedding under 75 people.
- **The Lobby** — anchored by a trickling Spanish fountain, it can host up to 150 guests for a cocktail-hour or reception layout.
- **Majorca, Catalina & Valencia Rooms** — additional flexible spaces for ceremonies, welcome events, or breakout gatherings.

Across all its spaces, the hotel can flex from an elopement of two up to a 500-guest event using multiple rooms simultaneously. Because it's a downtown property, valet and self parking are both available but should be confirmed and communicated to guests in advance — Downtown Albuquerque's street parking is not a reliable backup plan on a Saturday night. Load-in for florists and rental companies typically routes through a service entrance rather than the main lobby, which is exactly the kind of detail a planner coordinates ahead of time so your vendors aren't improvising the morning of.

## Best photo spots

- The **rooftop lounge (Ibiza)**, with Sandia Mountain and city skyline views — the single most requested sunset photo spot at the property.
- The **lobby staircase and mezzanine**, with wrought-iron railings and the stained-glass skylight overhead for soft, even daylight portraits.
- The **Barcelona Ballroom's private terrace**, useful for both first-look photos and a quiet moment away from the reception.
- The **exterior facade on 2nd Street NW**, whose Spanish Revival architecture and neon-era signage give a distinctly Downtown Albuquerque backdrop that doesn't read like every other hotel wedding.
